\olsection{First Order Logic}

\begin{prob}

    Consider the !!{structure}~$\Struct M$ with domain 
    $\Domain{M}=\{1,2,3\}$ and interpretation
    $\Assign{F}{M}=\{1,3\}$, $\Assign{G}{M}=\{\langle1,1\rangle,\langle2,1\rangle,\langle2,2\rangle,\langle3,2\rangle\}$.
    Say whether the following formulas are true in $\Struct{M}$. No
    need to justify your answers.
    \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\lforall[x](\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}])$
    \item $\lforall[x][\lexists[y](\Atom{G}{x,x}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x})]$
    \end{enumerate}

    \begin{ans}
    \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\lforall[x](\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}])$ is
    true in $\Struct{M}$.

    \emph{Explanation. With $s(x)=2$ we have
    $\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[x][\Atom{G}{x,y}]$ true because
    $\Atom{F}{x}$ is false. With $s(x)=1$ or $s(x)=3$ we also have
    $\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[x][\Atom{G}{x,y}]$ true because we have
    $\lexists[x][\Atom{G}{x,y}]$ true: take $s(y)=1$ with $s(x)=1$ and
    $s(y)=2$ when $s(x)=3$. So
    $\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[x][\Atom{G}{x,y}]$ is true for any
    assignment to $x$, hence $\forall x(Fx\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}])$.} 
    
    \emph{If you need to justify.} Let $s$ be any !!{variable} 
    assignment. We have: 
    \begin{align*}
    \Sat{M}{\lexists[y][Gxy]}[\Subst{s}{1}{x}] 
        & \text{ since }\Sat{M}{Gxy}[\Subst{\Subst{s}{1}{x}}{1}{y}] 
    \\
        & \text{ since }\tuple{1,1}\in\Assign{G}{M}
    \\        
    \Sat/{M}{Fx}[\Subst{s}{2}{x}]
        & \text{ since }2\notin\Assign{F}{M}
    \\
    \Sat{M}{\lexists[y][Gxy]}[\Subst{s}{3}{x}] 
        & \text{ since }\Sat{M}{Gxy}[\Subst{\Subst{s}{3}{x}}{2}{y}] 
    \\
        & \text{ since }\tuple{3,2}\in\Assign{G}{M}
    \end{align*}
    From this we have $\Sat{M}{\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}]}[\Subst{s}{m}{x}]$
    for every $m\in\Domain{M}$, hence $\Sat{M}{\lforall[x](\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}])}[s]$.

    \item $\lforall[x][\lexists[y](\Atom{G}{x,x}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x})]$ is true in
    $\Struct{M}$. 

    \emph{If you need to justify.} Let $s$ be any !!{variable} 
        assignment. We have: 
        \begin{align*}
        \Sat{M}{\lexists[y](\Atom{G}{x,x}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x})}[\Subst{s}{1}{x}]
             & \text{ since }\Sat{M}{\Atom{G}{x,x}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x}}[\Subst{\Subst{s}{1}{x}}{1}{y}]
        \\
             & \text{ since }\Sat{M}{\Atom{G}{y,x}}[\Subst{\Subst{s}{1}{x}}{1}{y}]
        \\
        \Sat{M}{\lexists[y](\Atom{G}{x,x}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x})}[\Subst{s}{2}{x}]
             & \text{ since }\Sat{M}{\Atom{G}{x,x}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x}}[\Subst{\Subst{s}{2}{x}}{2}{y}]
        \\
             & \text{ since }\Sat{M}{\Atom{G}{y,x}}[\Subst{\Subst{s}{2}{x}}{2}{y}]
        \\
        \Sat{M}{\lexists[y](\Atom{G}{x,x}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x})}[\Subst{s}{3}{x}]
             & \text{ since }\Sat{M}{\Atom{G}{x,x}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x}}[\Subst{\Subst{s}{3}{x}}{1}{y}]
        \\
             & \text{ since }\Sat/{M}{\Atom{G}{x,x}}[\Subst{\Subst{s}{3}{x}}{1}{y}]
        \end{align*}
    \end{enumerate}
    From this we have $\Sat{M}{\lforall[x][\lexists[y](\Atom{G}{x,x}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x})]}[s]$. 

    For a smarter justification, you could notice that the formula is
    a logical truth because $\lexists[y](\Atom{G}{x,x}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x})$ is
    guaranteed to be satisfied relative to any assignment in any
    !!{structure}. For if $\Sat{M}{\Atom{G}{x,x}}[s]$, then
    $\Sat{M}{\Atom{G}{y,x}}[\Subst{s}{s(x)}{y}]$. Therefore either
    $\Sat/{M}{\Atom{G}{x,x}}[s]$ and
    $\Sat/{M}{\Atom{G}{x,x}}[\Subst{s}{s(x)}{y}]$ or
    $\Sat{M}{\Atom{G}{y,x}}[\Subst{s}{s(x)}{y}]$. Either way, 
    $\Sat{M}{\lexists[y](\Atom{G}{x,x}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x})}[s]$.
    Generalizing on $s$, $\Sat{M}{\lforall[x][\lexists[y](\Atom{G}{x,x}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x})]}$.

    % longish answer, clear page
    \clearpage
    \end{ans}

\end{prob}

\begin{prob}

    Formalize the following argument in first-order logic. Is the argument
    valid? If it is, give a natural deduction proof; otherwise a counterexample.
    \begin{quote}
    Every player followed some player whom Ali didn't see. 
    
    Ali saw every player who followed someone.
    
    Therefore: no player followed any player who followed someone.
    \end{quote}

    \begin{ans}
    There is an ambiguity in the first premise: we could read with 
    `some player' taking wide scope (there's some specific player Ali
    didn't see that every player followed). However, I find the 
    reading in which `some player' take narrow scope more natural.
    That is the reading I formalise. 
    
    Dictionary:

    \begin{tabular}{ll}
    $P$ & \dots is a player \\
    $S$ & \dots saw \dots \\
    $F$ & \dots followed \dots
    \end{tabular}
    
    Formalization:
    \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\lforall[x](\Atom{P}{x}\lif\lexists[y](\Atom{P}{y}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,y}\land\Atom{F}{x,y}))$
    \item $\lforall[x]((\Atom{P}{x}\land \lexists[y][\Atom{F}{x,y}])\lif\Atom{S}{a,x})$
    \item $\lnot\lexists[x](\Atom{P}{x}\land\lexists[y]((\Atom{P}{y}\land\lexists[z][\Atom{F}{y,z}])\land\Atom{F}{x,y}))$
    \end{enumerate}

    The argument is valid. Full !!{derivation} on the next page.
    Labels for non-discharging rules are omitted for reasons of space,
    see the break-down of the !!{derivation} in the commentary
    afterwards for full labels.

    Alternative answer: read `some player' with a narrow scope in 
    the first premise. Replace the formalisation of the first 
    premise with:
    $$
    \lexists[x](\Atom{P}{x}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,x}\land\lforall[y](\Atom{P}{y}\lif\Atom{F}{x,y}))
    $$
    The argument is valid under that reading too. In this case the 
    premises are inconsistent, so any conclusion follows. See

    \emph{Comment. We find the formalisations by first noticing that
    the original sentences are equivalent to the following,
    respectively:}
    \begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{``Every player followed some player whom Ali didn't
    see.'' For every player $x$, there's some $y$ such that $y$ is a
    player, Ali didn't see $y$, and $x$ followed $y$.}

    \emph{Or, alternatively: there's some specific player $x$ 
    such that Ali didn't see $x$ and such that 
    for every player $y$, $y$ followed $x$.}

    \item \emph{``Ali saw every player who followed someone.'' For
    every $x$ such that $x$ is a player and there's some $y$ $x$
    followed, Ali didn't see $x$}
    \item \emph{``No player followed any player who followed
    someone.'' There is no $x$ such that: $x$ is player and there is
    some $y$ that is a player, that followed someone, and that $x$
    followed.}

    \emph{Why not $\lnot\lexists[x](\Atom{P}{x}\land\lforall[y](\Atom{F}{x,y}\lif
    \Atom{P}{y}\land\lexists[z][\Atom{F}{y,z}]))$? That is pretty close
    but not quite right (would get most but not all the points). 
    For the conclusion isn't `no player followed
    \emph{every} player who...'. Compare `no player
    followed any cat' vs `no player followed every cat'. The first is
    false as soon as a player followed \emph{some} cat; the second is
    false only if some player failed to follow \emph{any of} the cats. 
    So we formalise `no player followed any cat' as 
    $\lnot\lexists[x](\Atom{P}{x}\land\lexists[y](\Atom{C}{y}\land Fxy))$ 
    and `no player followed every
    cat' as $\lnot\lexists[x](Px\land\lforall[y](Cy\lif Fxy))$. The same
    applies here.}
    \end{enumerate}

    \emph{The argument is valid whichever way we formalise 
    the first premise.}
    
    \emph{First formalisation (`some player' narrow scope in the
    first premise). The premises seem inconsistent, in which case the
    argument would be valid simply because contradictory premises
    entail everything. They are not quite inconsistent though; rather,
    they contradict the assumption that there are players at all. For
    suppose that there is some player, call it $b$: $\Atom{P}{b}$. By
    the first premise, $b$ followed some player that Ali didn't see:
    $\lexists[y](\Atom{P}{y}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,y}\land\Atom{F}{b,y})$.
    Call this player $c$: $\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot \Atom{S}{a,c}\land
    \Atom{F}{b,c}$. By the first premise again, since $c$ is a player,
    they followed someone: $\lexists[y][\Atom{F}{c,y}]$. By the second
    premise, since $\Atom{P}{b}\land\lexists[y][\Atom{F}{c,y}]$, Ali
    saw $c$: $\Atom{S}{a,c}$. But we had that $\lnot\Atom{S}{a,c}$,
    contradiction: so $\Atom{P}{b}$ was false, and in general, there
    is no player: $\lnot\lexists[x][\Atom{P}{x}]$. A fortiori there is no player
    that followed etc.: $\lnot\lexists[x](\Atom{P}{x}\land\ldots)$.}

    \emph{The proof essentially follows these steps.
    We assume $\Atom{P}{b}$ and seek to derive a contradiction. We
    then use that sub-proof as part of a \emph{reductio} of $\exists
    x(Px\land\ldots)$.}

    \emph{Our first job, then, is to work out a contradiction 
    from $\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot \Atom{S}{a,c}\land
    \Atom{F}{b,c}$
    together with our premises. The first step is to use
    the first premise to show that $c$ followed someone,
    since $c$ are a player. We first derive that they 
    followed someone that Ali didn't see:
    $\lexists[y](\Atom{P}{y}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,y}\land\Atom{F}{c,y})$:}
    \begin{prooftree}
        \AxiomC{$\lforall[x](\Atom{P}{x}\lif\lexists[y](\Atom{P}{y}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,y}\land\Atom{F}{x,y}))$}
        \RightLabel{\Elim{\lforall}}
        \UnaryInfC{$\Atom{P}{c}\lif\lexists[y](\Atom{P}{y}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,y}\land\Atom{F}{c,y})$}
            \AxiomC{$\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,c}\land\Atom{F}{b,c}$}
            \RightLabel{\Elim{\land}}
            \UnaryInfC{$\Atom{P}{c}$}
        \RightLabel{\Elim{\lif}}
        \BinaryInfC{$\lexists[y](\Atom{P}{y}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,y}\land\Atom{F}{c,y})$}
    \end{prooftree}
    \emph{From the claim that followed someone that Ali didn't
    see, we should be able to show that they followed someone.
    Indeed, we use one step of \Elim{\lexists}:}
    \begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{$\lexists[y](\Atom{P}{y}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,y}\land\Atom{F}{c,y})$}
        \AxiomC{$\Discharge{\Atom{P}{d}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,d}\land\Atom{F}{c,d}}{1}$}
        \RightLabel{\Elim{\land}}
        \UnaryInfC{$\Atom{F}{c,d}$}
        \RightLabel{\Intro{\lexists}}
        \UnaryInfC{$\lexists[y][\Atom{F}{c,y}]$}
    \DischargeRule{\Elim{\lexists}}{1}
    \BinaryInfC{$\lexists[y][\Atom{F}{c,y}]$}
    \end{prooftree}
    \emph{We put this together with another use of $\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,c}\land\Atom{F}{b,c}$
    to derive derive that $c$ is \emph{a player} who followed someone:}
    {\footnotesize
    \begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{$\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,c}\land\Atom{F}{b,c}$}
    \RightLabel{\Elim{\land}}
    \UnaryInfC{$\Atom{P}{c}$}
        \AxiomC{$\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,c}\land\Atom{F}{b,c}$, first premise}
        \DeduceC{$\lexists[y](\Atom{P}{y}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,y}\land\Atom{F}{c,y})$}
            \AxiomC{$\Discharge{\Atom{P}{d}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,d}\land\Atom{F}{c,d}}{1}$}
            \RightLabel{\Elim{\land}}
            \UnaryInfC{$\Atom{F}{c,d}$}
            \RightLabel{\Intro{\lexists}}
            \UnaryInfC{$\lexists[y][\Atom{F}{c,y}]$}
        \DischargeRule{\Elim{\lexists}}{1}
        \BinaryInfC{$\lexists[y][\Atom{F}{c,y}]$}
    \RightLabel{\Intro{\land}}
    \BinaryInfC{$\Atom{P}{c}\land\lexists[y][\Atom{F}{c,y}]$}
    \end{prooftree}
    }

    \emph{The second step is to use the second premise,
    together with the derived claim that $c$ is a 
    player who followed someone, to conclude that Ali saw $c$.
    That is straightforward:}
    \begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{$\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,c}\land\Atom{F}{b,c}$, first premise}
    \DeduceC{$\Atom{P}{c}\land\lexists[y][\Atom{F}{c,y}]$}
        \AxiomC{$\lforall[x]((\Atom{P}{x}\land \lexists[y][\Atom{F}{x,y}])\lif\Atom{S}{a,x})$}
        \RightLabel{\Elim{\lforall}}
        \UnaryInfC{$(\Atom{P}{c}\land \lexists[y][\Atom{F}{c,y}])\lif\Atom{S}{a,x}$}
    \RightLabel{\Elim{\lif}}
    \BinaryInfC{$\Atom{S}{a,c}$}
    \end{prooftree}
    
    \emph{We've thus shown that from the assumption that $c$ is a 
    player whom Ali didn't see and who $b$ followed,
    $\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,c}\land\Atom{F}{b,c}$, and our premises
    it follows that Ali saw $c$. But of course it also 
    followed that Ali didn't see $c$. We therefore put
    the previous together with yet another use 
    of the assumption $\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,c}\land\Atom{F}{b,c}$
    to get a contradiction:}
    \begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{$\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,c}\land\Atom{F}{b,c}$}
    \RightLabel{\Elim{\land}}
    \UnaryInfC{$\lnot\Atom{S}{a,c}$}
    \AxiomC{$\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,c}\land\Atom{F}{b,c}$, both premises}
        \DeduceC{$\Atom{S}{a,c}$}
    \RightLabel{\Intro{\bot}}
    \BinaryInfC{$\bot$}
    \end{prooftree}
    \emph{Our first job is done: getting a contradiction
    out of $\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot \Atom{S}{a,c}\land
    \Atom{F}{b,c}$ together with the argument's premises.}

    \emph{The second job is to derive a contradiction
    from the assumption that there is a player ($b$), 
    using the result of our first job and (again) the
    first premises. The first step is to generalize the
    previous result over $c$: if there is 
    \emph{some} player that Ali didn't see that followed someone, 
    a contradiction follows:}
    \begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{$\lexists[y](\Atom{P}{y}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,y}\land\Atom{F}{b,y})$}
        \AxiomC{$\Discharge{\Atom{P}{c}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,c}\land\Atom{F}{b,c}}{2}$, both premises}
        \DeduceC{$\bot$}
    \DischargeRule{\Elim{\lexists}}{2}
    \BinaryInfC{$\bot$}
    \end{prooftree}

    \emph{The second step is to use the first premise to show that 
    if there is a player $b$, then we have some player that Ali 
    didn't see that followed someone, from which we've 
    derived a contradiction:}
    \begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{$\Atom{P}{b}$}
        \AxiomC{$\lforall[x](\Atom{P}{x}\lif\lexists[y](\Atom{P}{y}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,y}\land\Atom{F}{x,y}))$}
        \RightLabel{\Elim{\lforall}}
        \UnaryInfC{$\Atom{P}{b}\lif\lexists[y](\Atom{P}{y}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,y}\land\Atom{F}{b,y})$}
    \RightLabel{\Elim{\lif}}
    \BinaryInfC{$\lexists[y](\Atom{P}{y}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,y}\land\Atom{F}{b,y})$}
        \AxiomC{both premises}
    \BinaryInfC{$\vdots$}\noLine
    \UnaryInfC{$\bot$}
    \end{prooftree}
    \emph{Our second job is complete. The !!{derivation} can be used to negate (by
    \emph{reductio} any hypothesis that entails that there is a
    player.}

    \emph{Finally, we use the !!{derivation} obtained in our second
    job to get our desired conclusion. We may abbreviate it the
    conclusion as $\lexists[x](\Atom{P}{x}\land\ldots)$ since the
    $\ldots$ part is irrelevant to the proof:}
    \begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{$\Discharge{\lexists[x](\Atom{P}{x}\land\ldots)}{4}$}
        \AxiomC{$\Discharge{\Atom{P}{b}\land\ldots}{3}$}
        \RightLabel{\Elim{\land}}
        \UnaryInfC{$\Atom{P}{b}$}
            \AxiomC{both premises}
        \BinaryInfC{$\vdots$}
        \noLine
        \UnaryInfC{$\bot$}
    \DischargeRule{\Elim{\lexists}}{3}
    \BinaryInfC{$\bot$}
    \DischargeRule{$\bot_C$}{4}
    \UnaryInfC{$\lnot\lexists[x](\Atom{P}{x}\land\ldots)$}    
    \end{prooftree}
    
    \emph{This comcludes the proof. Note that the only undischarged
    assumptions are the argument's premises.}

    \emph{Second formalisation (`some player' wide scope in the first
    premise). Here the premises are inconsistent, for the first
    premise on its own entails that there is a player (call them $b$)
    that followed someone, but that Ali didn't see. The second premise
    tells us that any such player is seen by Ali: contradiction. The
    main difficulty is to work out the mechanics to derive
    $\lnot\Atom{S}{a,b}$ from
    $\Atom{P}{b}\land\lnot\Atom{S}{a,b}\land\lforall[y](\Atom{P}{y}\lif\Atom{F}{b,y})$
    I won't detail this here; the steps are slightly easier than 
    in other !!{derivation}. See the model answer above.
    }

    \clearpage
    \end{ans}
\end{prob}

\begin{prob}
    Give natural deduction !!{derivation}s of the following.
    \begin{enumerate}
    \item $\lforall[x](\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}]),
    \lforall[x][\lforall[y](\Atom{G}{x,y}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x})]
    \Proves\lforall[x](\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{y,x}])$
    \item $\lexists[x][\Atom{G}{a,x}]
    \Proves\lforall[x](\eq[x][a]\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}])$
    \end{enumerate}

    \begin{ans}
    !!^{derivation} of $\lforall[x](\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}]),
    \lforall[x][\lforall[y](\Atom{G}{x,y}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x})]
    \Proves\lforall[x](\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{y,x}])$
    
    \begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{$\Discharge{\Atom{F}{a}}{2}$}
        \AxiomC{$\lforall[x](\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}])$}
        \RightLabel{\Elim{\lforall}}
        \UnaryInfC{$\Atom{F}{a}\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{a,y}]$}
    \RightLabel{\Elim{\lif}}
    \BinaryInfC{$\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{a,y}]$}
        \AxiomC{$\Discharge{\Atom{G}{a,b}}{1}$}
            \AxiomC{$\lforall[x][\lforall[y](\Atom{G}{x,y}\lif \Atom{G}{y,x})]$}
            \RightLabel{\Elim{\lforall}}
            \UnaryInfC{$\lforall[y](\Atom{G}{a,y}\lif \Atom{G}{y,a})$}
            \RightLabel{\Elim{\lforall}}
            \UnaryInfC{$\Atom{G}{a,b}\lif\Atom{G}{b,a}$}
        \RightLabel{\Elim{\lif}}
        \BinaryInfC{$\Atom{G}{b,a}$}
        \RightLabel{\Intro{\lexists}}
        \UnaryInfC{$\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{y,a}]$}
    \DischargeRule{\Elim{\lexists}}{1}
    \BinaryInfC{$\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{y,a}]$}
    \DischargeRule{\Intro{\lif}}{2}
    \UnaryInfC{$\Atom{F}{a}\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{y,a}]$}
    \RightLabel{\Intro{\lforall}}
    \UnaryInfC{$\lforall[x](\Atom{F}{x}\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{y,x}])$}
    \end{prooftree}

    !!^{derivation} of 
    $\lexists[x][\Atom{G}{a,x}]
    \Proves\lforall[x](\eq[x][a]\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}])$

    \begin{prooftree}
    \AxiomC{$\lexists[x][\Atom{G}{a,x}]$}
        \AxiomC{$\Discharge{\Atom{G}{a,b}}{2}$}
            \AxiomC{$\Discharge{\eq[c][a]}{1}$}
        \RightLabel{\Elim{\eq}}
        \BinaryInfC{$\Atom{G}{c,b}$}
        \RightLabel{\Intro{\lexists}}
        \UnaryInfC{$\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{c,y}]$}
        \DischargeRule{\Intro{\lif}}{1}
        \UnaryInfC{$\eq[c][a]\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{c,y}]$}
    \DischargeRule{\Elim{\lexists}}{2}
    \BinaryInfC{$\eq[c][a]\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{c,y}]$}
    \RightLabel{\Intro{\lforall}}
    \UnaryInfC{$\lforall[x](\eq[x][a]\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}])$}

    \end{prooftree}

    \emph{Comment. The \Elim{\lexists} step is legit because 
    we've instantiated $x$ with a new constant, $b$, that doesn't
    appear anywhere else, and we've derived a formula 
    that doesn't contain $b$. The 
    \Intro{\forall} step is legit because when we reach it 
    the assumption $\eq[c][a]$ has been discharged, so 
    $c$ doesn't then appear in undischarged assumptions 
    above that step. In $\eq[c][a]$ must use a constant $c$ distinct
    from both $a$ and $b$: if we used $b$ instead 
    the \Elim{\lexists} would not be legit and if we used
    $a$ the \Intro{\lforall} step could only conclude 
    $\lforall[x](\eq[x][x]\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}])$.}
    
    \emph{The \Intro{\lforall} step can be placed after
    as well as before the \Elim{\lexists} one. The 
    following is correct too:}

    \begin{prooftree}
        \AxiomC{$\lexists[x][\Atom{G}{a,x}]$}
            \AxiomC{$\Discharge{\Atom{G}{a,b}}{2}$}
                \AxiomC{$\Discharge{\eq[c][a]}{1}$}
            \RightLabel{\Elim{\eq}}
            \BinaryInfC{$\Atom{G}{c,b}$}
            \RightLabel{\Intro{\lexists}}
            \UnaryInfC{$\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{c,y}]$}
            \DischargeRule{\Intro{\lif}}{1}
            \UnaryInfC{$\eq[c][a]\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{c,y}]$}
            \RightLabel{\Intro{\lforall}}
            \UnaryInfC{$\lforall[x](\eq[x][a]\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}])$}
        \DischargeRule{\Elim{\lexists}}{2}
        \BinaryInfC{$\lforall[x](\eq[x][a]\lif\lexists[y][\Atom{G}{x,y}])$}
    \end{prooftree}


    \end{ans}
\end{prob}
